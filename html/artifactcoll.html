Dr. Elira Voss

Cryptoarchaeologist | Ph.D. in Archaeology

Dr. Elira Voss is a cryptoarchaeologist with a Ph.D. from the University of Leiden, where she trained under mytho-materialist Dr. Yvan Mehren. She specializes in uncovering physical traces of legendary beings—such as dragon ossuaries, siren artifacts, and cultic lunar altars—bridging myth and material culture through stratigraphic excavation and multispectral scanning. With over a decade of experience across five continents, she’s known for her bold field intuition, meticulous artifact cataloging, and use of LIDAR and sonar tech in submerged dig sites. At the Mythical Creature Museum, Dr. Voss leads expedition planning and curates the Hidden Realms Unearthed exhibit. She is the recipient of the Sothis Award for Innovative Archaeological Method and traces her fascination with forgotten lore to her North African–Dutch heritage. Dr. Voss believes mythical relics are not proof of fantasy, but evidence of what a culture needed to believe to survive.

Dr. Thorne Calder

Mythological Ethnoarchaeologist | Ph.D. in Anthropology

Dr. Thorne Calder is a mythological ethnoarchaeologist with a Ph.D. in Anthropology from the University of Tübingen, where he trained under symbolic ritualist Dr. Anja Roth. Blending traditional excavation with cultural storytelling, he specializes in the material remnants of myth—tracking how legends like dragons, lycans, and frost-spirits manifest across global belief systems. Skilled in field stratigraphy, oral transcription, and drone-based terrain mapping, Dr. Calder brings a meticulous and quietly intuitive style to his work. At the Mythical Creature Museum, he oversees remote site analysis and leads field teams cataloging unearthed relics tied to creature lore. A recipient of the Valemont Folklore Research Grant, his work is shaped by his Métis-Celtic heritage and deep respect for ancestral memory. Dr. Calder believes truth in myth lies not in fact, but in function—in how stories teach us to fear, revere, and survive.

Rhett Alaric

Museum Interpreter | Cultural Narrative Specialist

Rhett Alaric is a mythosymbologist specializing in the symbolic analysis of mythological creature artifacts. With roots in Romani-Greek traditions, he explores how ancient societies used mythical beings to structure belief, power, and fear. His work blends cultural semiotics, decolonial museology, and immersive storytelling, often challenging traditional museum narratives. Alaric collaborates with artists and other researchers to reconstruct rituals and performative myths, particularly through hybrid relics like dragon remains and transformation masks. A self-described provocateur, he advocates for repatriation, plural interpretation, and the museum as a space of living cultural dialogue.
