Lore of the Sirens: The Veilmaidens

Origin

Long before the first sailors dared chart the ocean’s edge, the world was still raw with grief. From this grief—born of gods, drowned lands, and forgotten songs—came the Veilmaidens, beings we now call sirens. They were not born of flesh, but of sound. The oldest myths speak of them as divine harmonies made manifest, woven from the final cries of perished worlds and the lullabies of dying stars.

It is said the first siren sang not to lure, but to mourn. Her name was Elureth, and her voice was so sorrowful that even time paused to listen. Her song shaped the tides, drawing the ocean inward like breath drawn in anguish. When her lament touched the ears of mortals, their hearts shattered with longing—not for her, but for all they had lost and forgotten.

Nature and Purpose

Sirens are not monsters in the traditional sense. They are echoes of memory, bound to the sea not as prisoners, but as stewards of its secrets. Every siren is a keeper of some ancient sorrow—a drowned kingdom, a sunken love, a war no one remembers. Their songs are not traps, but truths, and it is the unbearable beauty of those truths that drives mortals to madness.

They sing to test the soul, not just to seduce the flesh. Those who can listen and survive are sometimes gifted glimpses of knowledge: lost names, ancestral wisdom, or visions of lives they never lived but somehow remember.

The Three Orders

Scholars of myth classify sirens into three ancient Orders, each tied to a different aspect of oceanic emotion:

  1. The Mourning Tide – Pale and cloaked in kelp, these sirens sing of love lost and homes forgotten beneath the waves. Their voices draw sailors not to death, but to remembrance—though many drown trying to hold onto what was never theirs.
  2. The Laughing Deep – Tricksters and shape-changers, these sirens use song to distort time and perception. Their laughter echoes in sea caves and whirlpools. They offer illusions of joy, but only to reveal the hollow hunger beneath.
  3. The Silent Wake – The rarest and most feared. They do not sing but radiate stillness. Their presence drains sound from the air and light from the water. It is said a Silent Wake siren is mourning something even the gods chose to forget.

Modern Echoes

Legends say that if you hear music at sea without wind or crew, you must cover your ears—not to block the danger, but to respect the sorrow. Sailors leave offerings of silver and stories carved in driftwood, hoping the Veilmaidens will spare their memories. Some dreamers claim they’ve heard sirens in sleep: not seductive, but familiar—as if someone they once loved long ago was calling from beneath the waves. Others say the sirens now watch from beneath oil-slicked waters and coral graveyards, their songs turning cold, bitter, and sharp.

They are not extinct. Only forgotten.
